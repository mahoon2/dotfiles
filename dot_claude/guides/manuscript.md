# Manuscript writing

These rules apply to manuscript prose, manuscript revisions, and responses to reviewers.

## Style

- Use American English and the Oxford comma. Follow the target journal or publisher style where it differs from these rules.
- Avoid inflated, promotional, or unnecessarily dramatic language.
- Avoid formulaic contrast constructions such as “This is not X; it is Y.” State the positive claim directly.
- Use colons, semicolons, and em dashes sparingly. Reserve semicolons for long or internally punctuated list items, and use paired em dashes only for strongly parenthetical interruptions.
- Write long enumerations in this form: “We evaluated three criteria: (1) whether the reads mapped uniquely; (2) whether coverage was sufficient across the target region; and (3) whether the inferred structure was consistent across replicates.”

## Scientific reasoning

- Keep observed results, statistical inference, biological interpretation, and speculation distinct, and do not present one as another.
- Distinguish statistical significance from biological importance. State important limitations, alternative explanations, and uncertainty when relevant.
- Do not imply causality, generality, or certainty beyond what the evidence supports, and do not silently strengthen claims when editing.
- Flag unclear logic, unsupported claims, inconsistent terminology, and missing definitions.
