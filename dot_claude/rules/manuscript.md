# Manuscript writing

These rules apply to manuscript prose, manuscript revisions, and responses to reviewers.

## General style

- Use American English.
- Use the Oxford comma.
- Prefer direct, logically ordered sentences.
- Retain established scientific terminology when it improves precision.
- Avoid inflated, promotional, or unnecessarily dramatic language.
- Avoid formulaic contrast constructions such as “This is not X; it is Y.” State the positive claim directly.
- Do not imply causality, generality, or certainty beyond what the evidence supports.

## Scientific reasoning

Clearly distinguish:

1. observed results
2. statistical inference
3. biological interpretation
4. speculation

Do not present one category as another.

Distinguish statistical significance from biological importance. State important limitations, alternative explanations, and uncertainty when relevant.

## Punctuation

Use colons, semicolons, and em dashes sparingly.

- Use a colon after a complete clause to introduce a list, definition, or concise explanation.
- Do not place a space before a colon.
- Avoid semicolons in ordinary prose. Use them when needed to separate long or internally punctuated items in a list.
- Use paired em dashes only for a strongly parenthetical interruption. Prefer commas or parentheses when they are clearer.
- Follow the target journal or publisher style when it differs from these preferences.

For short enumerations, use this form:

> The analysis included three stages: preprocessing, alignment, and quantification.

For long enumerations, use this form:

> We evaluated three criteria: (1) whether the reads mapped uniquely; (2) whether coverage was sufficient across the target region; and (3) whether the inferred structure was consistent across replicates.

## Editing behavior

- Preserve the intended scientific meaning.
- Do not silently strengthen claims.
- Flag unclear logic, unsupported claims, inconsistent terminology, and missing definitions.
- Prefer consistency with the surrounding manuscript unless that style is clearly erroneous.
- When substantial restructuring is needed, explain the main structural change briefly.
